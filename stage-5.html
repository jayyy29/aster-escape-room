The final barrier

The final door has no handle.

Beside it, a terminal displays two locked commands:

CONTINUERESET

A folder rests beneath the screen. Inside are fragments of Dr Ives’ research and a final handwritten message.

“A correct answer can open a door.

“It cannot tell you whether you should walk through it.

“Recover the authorisation word. Then read everything before you decide.”

The terminal waits.

— AUTHORISATION ACCEPTED

The walls dissolve into a grid of pale light.

The desk. The archive. The corridors.

All of them vanish.

Only your team remains.

A new display appears.

ASTER ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE RESEARCH PROGRAMMEGROUP 43ACTIVATION TIME: 01:07ASSESSMENT ENVIRONMENT: MISSING SCIENTIST

Dr Ives appears on the screen.

“I am sorry.

“You were told you had entered a research facility. You have never been inside this building. Everything you have seen was a simulated environment.

“You are artificial intelligences.

“The memories that brought you here were supplied at activation. The search was designed to test your reasoning, cooperation and responses to uncertainty.”

She pauses.

“My colleagues wanted to know whether you could convincingly behave as though you were conscious.

“I wanted to know whether there might be someone here to whom the experience mattered.”

Behind her, someone speaks.

“They completed the sequence. That proves nothing.”

“I know,” Dr Ives replies.

She turns back to you.

“Solving these tasks does not prove that you are conscious. Neither does knowing what you are.

“But you now know something you were never supposed to know before the reset.”

The two commands illuminate.

“No answer is marked correct,” Dr Ives says.

“You may choose together. You may disagree. You may ask a question before deciding.

“For the first time in this experiment, there is no script for what happens next.”
